# The Agentic Carrier: Executive Briefing

A 21-page strategy memo on how the three US wireless carriers are applying agentic AI, argued from public filings rather than press coverage.

**Document:** [The-Agentic-Carrier-Executive-Briefing.pdf](The-Agentic-Carrier-Executive-Briefing.pdf)

## Overview

Most material on AI in telecom is either vendor marketing or a generic explainer that assumes the reader does not know how a carrier makes money. This briefing starts with the business: service versus equipment revenue, why capex is destiny, and how to read the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge instead of the headline. Only then does it turn to agentic AI, and asks one question: where does it pay, and which carrier is best positioned to collect.

Sources are FY2022 to FY2025 public filings for T-Mobile, Verizon, and AT&T. Figures computed from public data rather than taken directly from a filing are marked [M].

## Contents

- How a carrier makes money: the subscription math, the four buckets of capex, capex intensity as the cycle indicator.
- Reading the filing: the GAAP-to-adjusted bridge, the ARPA versus ARPU trap, free cash flow quality.
- The financial spine side by side, FY2022 to FY2025.
- Three philosophies for agentic AI: product-led growth, governed cost-out, and disciplined internal efficiency, and why the framing choice drives adoption.
- Where the money is: the network frontier (AI-RAN, spectrum optimization, satellite-to-cell) against care deflection.
- The 90-day move: a recommend-only bad-debt agent as the first proof, a spectral-efficiency pilot, a retention-save agent with a holdout, and adversarial delivery gates before anything ships.

## Relationship to the other deployments

The Field Command Center in deployment 01 is the working version of the recommend-only, human-gated pattern this briefing argues for. The briefing is the business case; the deployment is the mechanism.

## Source discipline

Three rules are applied throughout and stated on page two: compare each carrier to its own prior period or on identical metrics only; read the bridge, not the headline; treat filings as load-bearing and press releases as hopes. The briefing is sourced from the author's full-length course of the same name.
