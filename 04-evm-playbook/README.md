# Earned Value Management Playbook

A practitioner briefing on Earned Value Management, from first principles to proactive, AI-enabled program control.

**Document:** [EVM-Playbook.pdf](EVM-Playbook.pdf)

## Overview

Earned Value Management is the program-control discipline that ties scope, schedule, and cost to one measured baseline. This playbook sets out the principles, the measures, and the operating rhythm a program needs before any automation is layered on top, and then describes where AI can extend the control loop without weakening it.

Grounded in EIA-748, the NDIA IPMD guides, and NASA EVM guidance. Frameworks are cited to source.

## Contents

- Why spending is not progress, and why variances are signals rather than verdicts.
- Baseline integrity: replan the future, never rewrite the past.
- The core measures and how to read them together: planned value, earned value, actual cost, schedule and cost variance, performance indices, estimate at completion, and estimate to complete.
- Control accounts, work packages, and the role of the control account manager.
- Agile programs under EVM: how to keep the baseline honest when scope is delivered in increments.
- Proactive control: continuous variance analysis, trend detection, and forecasting, including favorable trends as opportunities rather than only threats.
- Where AI helps and where it must not: drafting variance narratives and forecasts for human review, never replacing the baseline or the sign-off.

## Relationship to the other deployments

The same principle runs through this playbook and the three Agio Summit deployments: a measure is only useful if it is tied to a controlled baseline, recorded when it happens, and reviewed by a named owner. The evidence ledgers, budget guards, and human gates in deployments 01 to 03 are that principle applied to agentic software.
